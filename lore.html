Ogham

Ogham and the tree months

Ogham is an early medieval alphabet. Its letters are groups of short strokes cut across a line, usually the edge of a standing stone, and the oldest surviving inscriptions date from about the 4th to the 6th centuries. Each letter has an Irish name, and later medieval writers linked many of those names to trees: beith to birch, dair to oak, coll to hazel.

The thirteen-month tree calendar is much newer. The poet Robert Graves set it out in The White Goddess in 1948, building on that old letter-and-tree lore. It is a poet's calendar, not an ancient Druid one, and it is still a lovely way to walk through the year.
