The Gold in the Kyle

A story in letters from a Highland sea inlet, 1746

Spring 1746. The Jacobite rising is in its last months, and in Mackay Country, on the far north coast of Scotland, most of the clan has sided with the government. Then a ship carrying French gold for Prince Charles Edward Stuart's army runs aground in the Kyle of Tongue, and not every coin is accounted for.

Isobel Mackay starts writing letters: to her brother Alasdair, to people she trusts, and to one man she shouldn't. A romance, a mystery and the '45 seen from the other side.

What's a kyle? A narrow sea inlet or strait, from the Gaelic caol, "narrow". The Kyle of Tongue runs deep into the north coast of Sutherland.

Brother,

You told me to leave it alone, and I meant to. But the tide went out this morning and I saw what it left behind.

How it works

One story, twelve envelopes

  • Twelve installments, mailed once a month to addresses in the United States
  • Letters written in period voice but easy to read, with the kind of papers a 1746 household might keep tucked in among them
  • The whole story is written before the first envelope goes out, so it won't stall halfway
  • A short note in each envelope on what's real history and what's our invention
  • Paid once, up front. No automatic renewal, no surprise charges, and a gift costs what it says

The story is fiction set inside real events. Where a detail is history, we say so and say where it comes from. Isobel, Alasdair and Ruairidh are ours.

How we make it: any art in the envelopes is made with digital tools, including AI image tools, and edited by hand. The letters are written by W. Kroboth.
